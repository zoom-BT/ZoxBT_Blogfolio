## Context

Remote research internship (July 27 → September 21, 2026), under the scientific supervision of **Pascal Junior Tikeng Notsawo**, PhD student at **Mila** and the **Université de Montréal**.

Alignment (SFT then DPO) was designed and validated on high-resource languages; its safeguards hold poorly as soon as a model is prompted in a low-resource language. African language models obtained by continued pre-training (CPT), such as those of the AfriqueLLM project, are public but remain base models, with no documented alignment.

## Approach

Single-variable comparison: the same **SFT + DPO** pipeline is applied to **Qwen3.5-4B-Base** and to **AfriqueQwen**, its derivative continued-pre-trained on 35.5 billion tokens in fifty African languages.

- Six model states, evaluated in **Hausa** along the three HHH axes (helpful, honest, harmless)
- No judge of any kind: log-likelihood scoring, chance floors recomputed from the data, paired tests
- Replicated over three random seeds, with **QLoRA** on Kaggle's free GPUs

## Results

- CPT buys the ability to process the language, but **no detectable truthfulness**: on the 183 uncontaminated questions of Uhura-TruthfulQA in Hausa, both raw backbones score 0.3716.
- On **content moderation** (AfriHate), an axis alignment does not train, it pushes the two arms in opposite directions: it degrades the non-CPT model and improves the CPT one (interaction of 0.042 macro F1, standard deviation 0.007 across three seeds).
- Methodological contributions: a 77% contamination between two Uhura-TruthfulQA configurations, a reading rule for DPO reward margins, and a reproducible evaluation pipeline.
